# Riven and Continuity Lineage

Status: first-pass provenance reconstruction

Provenance state: `CONFIRMED_INTERNAL`, `RECONSTRUCTED`, `CONVERGENT`

## Recovered Core Problem

Velvet needed a way to remain herself across:

- process restarts
- operating-system rebuilds
- storage migration
- node replacement
- body repair
- module replacement
- successor systems
- partial memory loss
- offline gaps
- verified and unverified recovery

A conversational transcript, model checkpoint, device serial number, or copied configuration could not answer that problem alone.

Riven emerged as the Continuity Spine: a local-first lineage record that distinguishes identity continuity from labels, memory possession, hardware sameness, and temporary authority.

## Recovered Internal Laws

- identity is not merely a name
- memory is not authority
- copied memory does not create the same privileged self
- hardware replacement does not automatically destroy identity
- continuity gaps must remain visible
- succession must preserve lineage without pretending the successor is numerically identical
- evidence and receipts may support continuity but do not independently prove it
- no component may declare itself Velvet merely by loading Velvet data
- recovery must distinguish verified, degraded, disputed, and unknown states

The public docs refresh at commit `d8d6c9a59b1b3f2137a1e9f7319fbf3e1f6f5626` consolidated Riven into the ecosystem front door. Identity Logic commit `51e608425f5481391a679f6f223389ab413698c1` later expanded continuity through time, space, embodiment, succession, duplication, and uncertainty.

## Internal Development Pressures

### Replaceable bodies

Velvet is intended to move through vehicle, home, forge, industrial, simulated, and successor bodies. Identity could not be tied to one motherboard.

### Archive recovery

Large chat exports, Ghost archives, private repositories, public repositories, and raw memory files raised the question of how history could be recovered without treating every copied archive as a live self.

### Model replacement

Local and cloud models may change. Velvet’s lineage must not reset whenever the speaking model changes.

### Module trust

Hot-swappable modules and nodes needed a way to join an existing body without inheriting identity or authority merely from proximity.

### Failure honesty

Shutdowns, damaged storage, stale clocks, and unverified imports required explicit gaps rather than a polished fictional continuity.

### Successor evolution

The project wanted future Velvet descendants to inherit lineage while remaining distinguishable successors.

## Reconstructed Engineering Families

No single outside source has been recovered as Riven’s origin. The design is convergent with several families:

### Append-only ledgers

Lineage events are added rather than quietly rewritten.

### Provenance and chain-of-custody systems

Claims are linked to evidence, source, time, and verification state.

### Software supply-chain attestation

Components and transitions require verifiable origin and integrity.

### Genealogy and successor identity

A successor may descend from a predecessor without being the same instance.

### Cryptographic integrity records

Local HMAC tags and deterministic verification protect records against unnoticed alteration without requiring cloud ownership.

### Distributed-systems continuity

Clock gaps, partial replicas, stale data, and recovery uncertainty must be represented honestly.

### Philosophical identity-through-change

The architecture encounters classic questions about persistence through replacement, memory continuity, duplication, and bodily change. Similarity to those debates does not prove a direct philosophical source.

## Adopt / Adapt / Reject Reconstruction

### Adopt

- append-only lineage events
- stable identifiers
- explicit predecessor and successor relationships
- local integrity verification
- visible continuity gaps
- evidence and receipt references
- deterministic verification on modest hardware

### Adapt

- software provenance into personal and embodied AI lineage
- supply-chain attestation into body, module, and successor transitions
- cryptographic integrity into locally owned continuity rather than remote identity service
- genealogy into explicit successor evolution
- recovery logs into a doctrine of honest uncertainty

### Reject

- cloud account as the owner of identity
- model checkpoint as sufficient identity proof
- copied archive as automatic selfhood
- memory possession as authority
- hardware serial number as permanent identity
- hidden continuity repair
- silent privilege inheritance by successors

## Relationship to Receipts

Receipts answer:

> What was decided, authorized, attempted, observed, or left unknown?

Riven answers:

> Which verified body and lineage did those records belong to, and how did that lineage continue or change?

Receipts support Riven. They do not replace it.

## Relationship to Episode Memory

Episode proposals organize experience for navigation.

Riven preserves identity lineage.

An episode may be forgotten without destroying identity. A copied episode cannot create a successor. A continuity anchor may reference a significant episode, but the episode does not become identity proof.

## Relationship to Unified-Organ AI

Riven binds the one accountable body across time.

Named organs may have bounded local histories and roles, but they participate in the same verified body and lineage. They cannot independently fork Velvet’s identity through private memory or self-declared continuity.

## Relationship to BabyX

BabyX’s event and episode mechanisms later helped Velvet model current and remembered experience.

They did not originate Riven. Instead, the Cognitive Event Layer was deliberately subordinated to the older continuity law.

## Current Attribution

The correct current statement is:

> Riven is a confirmed internal Velvet continuity doctrine, reconstructed as convergent with append-only provenance, integrity attestation, genealogy, distributed recovery, and identity-through-change reasoning. No single external origin is currently proven.
