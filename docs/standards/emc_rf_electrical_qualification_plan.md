# Velvet EMC, RF, and Automotive Electrical Qualification Plan

**Status:** Living engineering qualification reference  
**Baseline:** EMC-RF-ELEC v0.1  
**Reviewed:** 2026-09-17  
**Compliance claim:** None  
**Purpose:** Give Eleanor a practical path from Velvet bench prototype to reproducible automotive electronic product without waiting until formal lab testing to discover avoidable EMC, RF, transient, ESD, grounding, or CAN-physical-layer problems.

## 1. Why this matters early

A Velvet board can be electrically unsafe, unreliable, radio-noisy, or vulnerable to vehicle disturbances without ever commanding steering or braking.

EMC and automotive electrical robustness therefore belong near the beginning of the official-board lifecycle, not at the end of a safety-automation programme.

The working rule is:

> **Design for qualification from revision zero; use inexpensive pre-compliance work to find ordinary mistakes; reserve formal test equipment and accredited/recognized labs for evidence that actually requires them.**

This plan does not reproduce proprietary standards text or invent test severities. Exact limits, pulse levels, dwell times, field strengths, setups and acceptance criteria must come from the applicable licensed/current standard, regulator, OEM/customer specification or qualified laboratory.

## 2. Current standards and regulatory landscape

### 2.1 UN Regulation No. 10 — vehicle electromagnetic compatibility

UNECE currently lists **UN Regulation No. 10, Revision 6** and Amendments 1 through 4.

R10 is a primary regulatory reference where vehicle EMC/type-approval requirements apply to vehicles and applicable electrical/electronic subassemblies.

Velvet relevance:

- official CAN gateways;
- wired I/O/control modules;
- vehicle audio/voice nodes;
- wireless gateways;
- driver-monitoring nodes;
- actuator/control ECUs;
- complete installed configurations where vehicle-level testing is required.

Primary source:
- https://unece.org/transport/vehicle-regulations-wp29/standards/addenda-1958-agreement-regulations-0-20

### 2.2 CISPR 25:2021 — emissions that can disturb on-board receivers

CISPR 25:2021 applies to vehicles and electronic/electrical components intended for vehicle use and covers conducted/radiated radio disturbances over a broad frequency range. Its purpose includes protecting on-board receivers such as broadcast radio, GNSS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and other radio services from disturbances generated by vehicle components.

This is particularly relevant to Velvet because the ecosystem intentionally places:

- computers;
- switching converters;
- Ethernet/PoE equipment;
- CAN interfaces;
- audio electronics;
- displays;
- cameras;
- GNSS;
- Wi-Fi/cellular equipment;
- and multiple local radios

inside one compact vehicle.

Primary source:
- https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/64645

### 2.3 ISO 11452-2:2019 — radiated RF immunity

ISO 11452-2:2019 specifies a component-level method for testing immunity to continuous narrowband radiated electromagnetic fields in an absorber-lined shielded enclosure.

A fourth edition is under development and is intended to replace the 2019 edition.

Velvet relevance:

- prove that an external RF field does not cause resets, false inputs, unsafe actuator requests, bus corruption or silent state changes;
- define safety-relevant functional-status expectations before lab testing;
- keep test harnesses/configurations reproducible.

Primary sources:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/68557.html
- https://www.iso.org/standard/92586.html

### 2.4 ISO 7637-2:2011 — supply-line transients

ISO 7637-2:2011 remains current after confirmation in 2025. It defines bench methods for electrical transients conducted along supply lines of equipment installed in 12 V and 24 V road vehicles. An amendment is under development.

Velvet relevance:

- ignition/load switching disturbances;
- supply transients;
- brownout/recovery behaviour;
- protection-device sizing;
- reset/data-corruption behaviour;
- explicit functional-status criteria during and after disturbances.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/50925.html

### 2.5 ISO 7637-3:2016 — transients coupled onto signal/control lines

ISO 7637-3:2016 remains current and addresses transient disturbances coupled onto lines other than supply lines.

Velvet relevance:

- CAN;
- GPIO;
- UART;
- sensor lines;
- button/owner-confirmation lines;
- actuator command/feedback lines;
- long harness branches.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/59603.html

### 2.6 ISO 10605:2023 — electrostatic discharge

ISO 10605:2023 is the current edition and was confirmed in 2025. It covers automotive ESD test methods for electronic modules and complete vehicles, including occupant/service/assembly discharge scenarios. An amendment is under development.

Velvet relevance:

- touch-accessible displays;
- USB/service connectors;
- metal enclosures;
- dashboard controls;
- harness/service connectors;
- cabin sensors;
- removable modules.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/79094.html

### 2.7 ISO 16750-2:2023 — automotive electrical loads

ISO 16750-2:2023 addresses electrical environmental loads for road-vehicle electronic equipment. It explicitly does **not** replace EMC testing.

Velvet relevance:

- supply-voltage operating windows;
- abnormal voltage conditions;
- startup/shutdown behaviour;
- vehicle wiring impedance;
- power-state transitions;
- protection/hold-up design.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/76119.html

### 2.8 ISO 16750 environmental family

The 2023 editions also provide useful product-engineering references for:

- ISO 16750-3: mechanical loads;
- ISO 16750-4: climatic loads;
- ISO 16750-5: chemical loads.

These matter once official Velvet electronics move from a friendly bench to a trunk, dashboard, engine-adjacent area, workshop, or other defined mounting location.

Primary sources:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/77579.html
- https://www.iso.org/standard/77580.html
- https://www.iso.org/standard/77581.html

### 2.9 ISO 11898-2:2026 — CAN high-speed physical layer

ISO 11898-2:2026, published May 2026, is the current fourth edition for high-speed CAN physical-medium attachment. It replaces the short-lived 2024 edition.

Velvet relevance:

- official CAN transceiver selection;
- termination/wake behaviour;
- low-power/selective-wake options where used;
- physical-layer compatibility;
- qualification of future CAN/CAN-FD-capable boards.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/90697.html

## 3. Canada product EMC / radio layer

### 3.1 ICES-003 Issue 7

ISED ICES-003 Issue 7 sets RF-emission limits, measurement methods and administrative requirements for information-technology equipment/digital apparatus, including devices using digital timing at or above its defined scope threshold.

A standalone Velvet compute/gateway/controller can therefore have Canadian RF-emission obligations independently of vehicle safety law.

Primary source:
- https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/interference-causing-equipment-standards/ices/ices-003-information-technology-equipment-including-digital-apparatus

### 3.2 RSS-Gen Issue 6

ISED published RSS-Gen Issue 6 on 2026-07-30. It is the current general framework for radio-apparatus compliance and includes updated listing, licensing, test-site and calibration requirements.

Primary source:
- https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/radio-equipment-standards/radio-standards-specifications-rss/rss-gen-general-requirements-compliance-radio-apparatus

### 3.3 RSS-247 Issue 4

RSS-247 Issue 4, published 2025-07-24, covers common licence-exempt digital transmission/frequency-hopping/WLAN devices in specified 902 MHz, 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands.

This is relevant to Wi-Fi/Bluetooth-class Velvet radios depending on the exact module/function/band.

Primary source:
- https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/radio-equipment-standards/radio-standards-specifications-rss/rss-247-digital-transmission-systems-dtss-frequency-hopping-systems-fhss-and-licence-exempt-local

### 3.4 Radio-module rule

Prefer certified/pre-certified radio modules where they satisfy the intended product and integration constraints, but do **not** treat the module's approval as blanket approval of the final Velvet product.

For every radio integration retain:

- exact module regulatory IDs;
- hardware/firmware revision;
- permitted antenna types/gains;
- antenna placement constraints;
- host integration instructions;
- RF exposure requirements if applicable;
- co-location/simultaneous-transmission assumptions;
- labeling/user-manual obligations;
- changes that would invalidate or require reassessment of the modular approval.

## 4. United States RF layer

FCC rules prohibit marketing/operation of non-exempt intentional or unintentional radiators that do not satisfy the applicable technical/administrative requirements and equipment-authorization procedure.

As a broad rule:

- intentional radiators generally use Certification;
- many unintentional radiators use Supplier's Declaration of Conformity;
- exact procedure depends on the device and rule part.

For a future Canada/U.S. Velvet product family, do not assume one country's RF approval automatically satisfies the other.

Primary references:
- https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-22-312A1.pdf
- https://apps.fcc.gov/oetcf/kdb/forms/FTSSearchResultPage.cfm?id=21079&switch=P

## 5. Velvet official-board electrical architecture rules

These are internal engineering rules, not quotations from the external standards.

### EMC-ELEC-001 — Protected vehicle-power entry

Every official vehicle-powered board shall have a documented protection strategy appropriate to its intended supply domain, including analysis of:

- input fuse/protection coordination;
- reverse-polarity conditions;
- over-voltage/transient suppression;
- undervoltage/brownout;
- filtering;
- inrush;
- ground/reference strategy;
- converter operating range;
- thermal consequences;
- safe recovery after disturbance.

### EMC-ELEC-002 — No unsafe state from power disturbance

A power transient, brownout, restart or recovery shall not itself create a new physical-control authorization.

After reset/recovery, safety-relevant outputs shall return to a defined inactive/degraded state until normal Runtime/Court/body validation is restored.

### EMC-ELEC-003 — Controlled shutdown is not protection

Velvet's timer/delay-off strategy may support orderly shutdown but shall not be treated as a substitute for hardware transient/reverse-polarity/over-voltage protection.

### EMC-IO-001 — Harness interface protection

Every external signal/control connector shall document:

- expected voltage/common-mode range;
- ESD/transient protection;
- pull-up/down/default state;
- open/short behaviour;
- ground/reference;
- cable length class;
- shielding/twisting requirement where relevant;
- whether the line is observation-only or authority-bearing.

### EMC-CAN-001 — CAN physical-layer dossier

Every official CAN interface shall retain:

- transceiver part/revision;
- ISO 11898-2 generation/profile claimed by the component;
- termination topology;
- stub/harness assumptions;
- common-mode/protection network;
- wake/sleep strategy;
- bus-off/recovery behaviour;
- passive/listen-only mode behaviour;
- voltage-domain isolation decision;
- ESD/transient strategy;
- test evidence.

### EMC-GND-001 — Grounding shall be designed, not improvised

For each board/enclosure define separately:

- power return;
- signal reference;
- chassis/shield bond;
- enclosure bond;
- cable-shield termination;
- high-current actuator return;
- noisy switching return;
- audio reference.

The design shall document where these join and why.

### EMC-HARNESS-001 — Harness is part of the product

For EMC purposes the board is not the whole product.

Record:

- connector family;
- wire gauge;
- pair twisting;
- shielding;
- branch/stub length;
- fuse location;
- ground point;
- physical routing assumptions;
- separation from ignition/motor/alternator/high-current lines;
- approved maximum/minimum harness variants.

### EMC-RF-001 — RF identity follows the shipped configuration

Radio compliance evidence shall bind to the actual shipped module, firmware, antenna and host configuration rather than a generic statement that "the module is certified."

### EMC-BOM-001 — Qualified BOM control

Parts that materially influence EMC/electrical behaviour shall be treated as controlled substitutions.

Examples include:

- DC/DC converter;
- inductors;
- common-mode chokes;
- TVS/protection parts;
- CAN transceiver;
- crystals/oscillators;
- RF module;
- antenna;
- enclosure/coatings;
- connector;
- cable/harness.

A sourcing substitution requires engineering review rather than silent replacement.

## 6. Functional-status classes for Velvet tests

Before any immunity/transient/ESD test, define the expected behaviour of the function under test.

Use an internal scheme such as:

- **V-A:** performs normally throughout and after exposure;
- **V-B:** temporary bounded degradation is permitted during exposure; automatic recovery afterward;
- **V-C:** temporary function interruption is permitted; deliberate recovery/reset may be required afterward;
- **V-D:** protected shutdown is permitted and human/service recovery is required;
- **V-X:** any uncontrolled actuation, authority escalation, corrupted safety state, unsafe bus transmission or unrecoverable loss of evidence is unacceptable.

The external test programme must map these internal states to the actual functional-performance criteria required by the applicable standard/customer/OEM specification.

For safety-relevant outputs, **unsafe motion/action is never an acceptable degradation class**.

## 7. Low-cost pre-compliance ladder

Pre-compliance does not establish regulatory compliance. Its job is to cheaply expose mistakes before expensive testing.

### Stage 0 — design review before PCB order

Eleanor checks:

- power-tree operating margins;
- transient-protection topology;
- protection-component ratings;
- connector pinout/fault cases;
- return-current paths;
- partitioning of noisy/high-current and sensitive circuits;
- switch-node geometry;
- decoupling placement;
- oscillator/RF routing;
- CAN placement/termination;
- shield/chassis strategy;
- reserved filter footprints;
- test points;
- BOM alternatives.

**Rule:** leave stuffing options for ferrites/common-mode chokes/RC/filter networks where practical. Empty pads are cheaper than a board respin.

### Stage 1 — ordinary bench electrical abuse, current limited

Using controlled bench equipment:

- verify nominal operating range;
- controlled undervoltage/brownout sweeps;
- controlled power cycling;
- startup with realistic connected loads;
- shutdown/key-off sequencing;
- current-limit/short-circuit response where safe and designed for it;
- reverse-polarity verification only through the board's intended protected input method;
- repeated reset/recovery testing;
- verify no unauthorized outputs during boot/brownout/reset.

Do not improvise high-energy automotive transient generators from loose inductors/relays as a substitute for a rated simulator.

### Stage 2 — noise hunting

Useful development tools can include:

- oscilloscope with appropriate probes;
- current probe where available;
- differential probe for floating/high-noise measurements;
- near-field E/H probes;
- spectrum analyzer or suitable development receiver;
- LISN appropriate to the intended pre-compliance method;
- CAN analyzer;
- logic analyzer for correlation with resets/errors;
- programmable DC supply.

Goals:

- identify switching harmonics;
- find noisy cables/connectors;
- correlate RF/noise with firmware operating modes;
- identify converter, display, Ethernet, camera and clock sources;
- verify filters/chokes actually improve rather than relocate the problem.

### Stage 3 — vehicle-representative harness bench

Test the real:

- harness length;
- connector family;
- enclosure;
- ground strategy;
- CAN topology;
- radio antenna;
- display/camera peripherals;
- worst-case processor/network activity;
- audio amplifier operation;
- actuator/switching loads appropriate to the product.

A tiny PCB on short bench wires is not a representative EMC configuration.

### Stage 4 — controlled disturbance pre-tests

Use appropriate commercial/rated equipment or a competent pre-compliance lab for:

- automotive supply transient screening;
- signal-line transient coupling;
- ESD;
- bulk/radiated immunity screening;
- conducted/radiated emissions screening.

The test configuration and software operating mode shall be versioned and receipted.

### Stage 5 — formal qualification

Once the applicable market/product path is known, obtain the exact current standard/regulatory requirements and test through the appropriate competent/recognized/accredited facility where formal evidence is required.

Do not market Stage 0-4 engineering checks as certification.

## 8. Worst-case operating modes

Every official board shall have defined worst-case EMC modes.

Examples:

### Compute/gateway board

- CPU/network load high;
- all Ethernet links active;
- storage read/write;
- CAN receive/transmit where allowed by the test;
- display output active;
- USB peripherals active;
- DC/DC converters at representative worst-case loading.

### Audio node

- all digital audio clocks active;
- amplifier output at defined representative load;
- microphones active;
- Ethernet/network activity;
- local speech processing active.

### Wireless gateway

- highest permitted transmit duty/use case;
- simultaneous network traffic where supported;
- GNSS receive;
- vehicle bus traffic;
- DC/DC worst-case load.

### I/O/actuator controller

- maximum representative output switching;
- inductive loads through production suppression network;
- CAN and diagnostics active;
- sensor inputs exercised;
- fault detection running.

No formal test result should be treated as covering a materially quieter configuration than the actual product without engineering justification.

## 9. Vehicle-installation zoning

For each installation define a noise/sensitivity map.

Candidate zones:

- **Zone A — RF/sensitive:** GNSS antennas, microphones, analog audio, low-level sensors;
- **Zone B — digital:** SBCs, Ethernet switch, displays, storage;
- **Zone C — switching power:** DC/DC, PoE conversion, amplifiers;
- **Zone D — inductive/high-current:** relays, motors, actuators, heaters;
- **Zone E — vehicle network:** CAN/gateway harness;
- **Zone F — intentional RF:** cellular/Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/other transmitters.

Routing and grounding should avoid casually mixing Zone A with C/D/F.

This is a design heuristic, not an external regulatory zoning scheme.

## 10. Product-specific first qualification targets

### Passive CAN reader

First priorities:

- protected power input;
- emissions;
- ESD;
- supply/signal transients;
- CAN physical-layer robustness;
- bus passivity under reset/fault.

### Wired I/O board

First priorities:

- supply transients;
- inductive-load suppression;
- signal-line transients;
- ESD;
- emissions from switching outputs/DC/DC;
- fail-safe output defaults;
- thermal/current fault behaviour.

### Audio/voice node

First priorities:

- emissions into on-board radio/GNSS;
- amplifier/converter noise;
- ESD at user/service surfaces;
- microphone/audio-ground isolation;
- immunity to cellular/Wi-Fi transmitters.

### Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/cellular gateway

First priorities:

- ISED/FCC radio-module integration;
- antenna/co-location conditions;
- host digital emissions;
- vehicle EMC;
- supply transients;
- cybersecurity/update identity.

### Driver-monitoring node

First priorities:

- camera/compute emissions;
- radiated immunity;
- ESD;
- power disturbances;
- false-output behaviour during disturbances;
- exact sensor/model/software version binding.

### Charlotte actuator controller

Treat as the highest tier:

- all above items;
- defined safety functional-status criteria;
- independent output inhibition;
- fault-injection;
- loss/corruption of CAN/sensor lines;
- power-transient behaviour under load;
- radiated immunity with no unsafe actuation;
- vehicle-level validation after component qualification.

## 12. Eleanor gates

Suggested future gates:

- **HW-EMC-ARCH-REVIEWED** — schematic/layout/harness EMC review complete;
- **HW-POWER-ABUSE-PASSED** — controlled bench power-state/fault tests passed;
- **HW-EMISSIONS-PRESCREENED** — development emissions screening complete;
- **HW-IMMUNITY-PRESCREENED** — development immunity screening complete;
- **HW-RF-INTEGRATION-LOCKED** — module/antenna/host regulatory configuration locked;
- **HW-CAN-PHY-REVIEWED** — CAN physical layer reviewed against current product requirements;
- **HW-FORMAL-QUALIFIED** — required formal tests completed for the declared market/product configuration.

A board may be a useful prototype without passing these gates. It shall not silently inherit a production-qualified status from a related prototype.

## 13. What changes for Velvet now

No manifesto change.

The practical change is that future **Velvet-specific plug-and-play boards** should be laid out from their first revision with:

- automotive power protection;
- EMC-conscious grounding/routing;
- reserved filter/protection footprints;
- reproducible harness definitions;
- controlled BOMs;
- test points;
- known safe boot/reset states;
- room for qualified radio-module integration;
- revision-linked evidence.

That costs very little while the board is still pixels in Eleanor's CAD workflow and can cost a great deal if discovered after manufacturing.
