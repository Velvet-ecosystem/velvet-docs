# Canada + British Columbia Baseline Snapshot — 2026-09-17 / CA-BC v0.1

**Record type:** Historical standards/applicability snapshot  
**Rewrite policy:** Immutable after merge except factual correction with explicit correction note.

## Why this snapshot exists

This snapshot records the first dedicated Canadian federal + British Columbia regulatory deep pass undertaken after Velvet's 2026-09-17 EU/international baseline.

Its purpose is to preserve what the project understood before a first commercial Velvet product was defined.

## Key findings at this date

1. Canada's Motor Vehicle Safety Act does not define every automotive accessory as "equipment." The statutory definition points to Schedule I, which currently lists tires and equipment for restraint of children/disabled persons.
2. A Velvet board can still become part of federal vehicle-safety analysis through the complete vehicle / pre-first-retail alteration path, so transaction and installation stage matter.
3. BC in-use roadworthiness remains independently relevant to an already-registered vehicle. A safety-relevant Velvet installation must preserve the complete vehicle's required brake/steering/other performance.
4. BC's modified/reconstructed-vehicle inspection rules have defined triggers; this review does not claim every Velvet retrofit automatically requires a modified-vehicle inspection.
5. Transport Canada's connected/automated-vehicle and cybersecurity material provides important voluntary engineering references for future Charlotte/Temperance/connected-gateway products.
6. PIPA-BC is central for private-sector personal-information handling within BC, while PIPEDA can remain relevant to interprovincial/international flows or other federal contexts.
7. Local-first processing has potential compliance value by reducing business custody of cabin/location/behavioural/health-adjacent information, but retention must still be purpose-specific.
8. CCPSA applicability cannot be assumed either way for every aftermarket Velvet electronic product; product classification must distinguish vehicle/integral parts from general consumer products.
9. The practical near-term business additions are classification records, intended-use statements, build/BOM identity, installation dossiers, privacy data-flow/retention records and a field incident/defect workflow.
10. No change to Velvet's core manifesto or architecture was identified as necessary by this review.

Future Canada/BC reviews should be able to show:

```text
pre-existing Velvet design
  -> 2026-09-17 Canada/BC baseline
  -> first commercial product classification
  -> product-specific requirements
  -> tests/evidence

```

This snapshot should not be rewritten to imply that later product requirements were already complete on 2026-09-17.
