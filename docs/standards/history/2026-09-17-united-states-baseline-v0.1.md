# United States Baseline Snapshot — 2026-09-17 / US v0.1

## Why this snapshot exists

This snapshot preserves Velvet's first dedicated U.S. regulatory/product deep pass before a first commercial U.S. Velvet product is defined.

It records the project's understanding of the U.S. split between federal vehicle safety, commercial installation, safety-defect/recall duties, emissions, RF authorization, consumer protection, privacy, repair/warranty law and state overlays.

## Key findings at this date

1. U.S. "motor vehicle equipment" is broad enough to include aftermarket accessories/additions; the absence of a directly applicable FMVSS does not necessarily remove the product from NHTSA safety-defect/recall jurisdiction.
2. 49 CFR Part 566 manufacturer identification applies to motor vehicles and to motor-vehicle equipment to which an FMVSS applies; it should not be applied indiscriminately to every aftermarket accessory.
3. Commercial installation and owner DIY are materially different under the federal "make inoperative" rule.
4. Pre-first-retail alteration of a certified vehicle is a separate certification role under 49 CFR 567.7.
5. Future Velvet motor-vehicle equipment needs field-defect/recall readiness even if no direct FMVSS applies.
6. EPA emissions/OBD requirements are a separate legal axis from NHTSA safety. Ruby/ECU work therefore needs an explicit emissions anti-tampering gate.
7. EPA's 2026 recognition of SEMA's Certified Emissions programme creates a current federal aftermarket-emissions pathway worth tracking, while California retains its own CARB aftermarket exemption process.
8. FCC authorization remains a separate product-market gate for applicable radio/digital devices.
9. FTC connected-vehicle enforcement makes purpose, consent, sharing and user control material business risks for cloud-connected vehicle data.
10. California CCPA may become relevant as business scale/data volume grows; Washington My Health My Data deserves early attention for Temperance-related health-adjacent data even for small businesses.
11. Magnuson-Moss/FTC warranty law fits Velvet's repairable/open ethos but requires disciplined warranty drafting.
12. The current DMCA Section 1201 vehicle exemption supports diagnosis, repair and lawful modification/access to vehicle operational data, but does not excuse DOT/EPA violations.
13. State law remains necessary for roadworthiness, installers, insurance, privacy and autonomous-driving tests/deployment.
14. No core manifesto change was identified as necessary.
