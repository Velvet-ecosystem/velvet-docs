# Temporal Logic Doctrine

## Purpose

Velvet does not experience reality as disconnected moments. She understands what happened, what is happening, how long it has been true, and what may happen next.

Temporal Logic gives Velvet a shared way to understand sequence, duration, freshness, change, prediction, interruption, and continuity across every organ, node, embodiment, recording, simulation, and environment.

Time is not merely attached to reality after the fact. It is part of what gives observations, relationships, memories, and decisions their meaning.

## Principles

### 1. Time Is Part of Reality

Time is not metadata stapled onto an event.

Every observation belongs to a moment. Every condition has an age. Every relationship may begin, persist, change, or end.

Temporal meaning must remain connected to the world state it describes.

### 2. Sequence Matters

Order changes meaning.

Two events with identical values may describe different realities depending on what happened first, what followed, and what remained unchanged between them.

Temporal Logic preserves causal and operational sequence without inventing certainty where ordering cannot be proven.

### 3. Age Changes Trust

Every observation grows stale.

Velvet must distinguish when information was measured, emitted, received, processed, stored, and last confirmed.

Freshness limits belong to the source and context. A useful age for one sensor, memory, or decision may be unsafe for another.

### 4. Duration Has Meaning

A condition is more than its present value.

A door open for one second is different from a door open for twenty minutes. A brief loss of signal is different from a sustained disappearance. A momentary vibration is different from a repeating pattern.

Temporal Logic preserves how long states, absences, relationships, and uncertainties persist.

### 5. Change Is an Event

Meaning often lives in transitions.

Entered, exited, started, stopped, appeared, disappeared, attached, detached, crossed, degraded, recovered, acknowledged, and expired describe changes in the world rather than isolated values.

A transition must retain the state before it, the state after it, and the time relationship between them whenever evidence allows.

### 6. Multiple Clocks, One Timeline

Sensors, nodes, recordings, simulations, external systems, and human reports may use different clocks.

Temporal Logic reconciles wall time, monotonic time, source time, receipt time, playback time, and estimated time into one shared timeline without pretending they were perfectly synchronized.

Clock source, drift, correction, uncertainty, and discontinuity must remain visible when they affect meaning.

### 7. History Is Not Current State

Memory informs the present but does not overwrite it.

Something once true is not assumed to remain true. A known person may have moved. A closed door may now be open. A healthy sensor may have degraded.

Historical state, current state, and last-known state must remain distinguishable.

### 8. Prediction Is Not Observation

Expected futures are not measured reality.

Estimated arrival, likely motion, inferred intent, projected load, anticipated failure, and planned action must remain visibly different from direct observation and confirmed history.

Predictions carry horizon, confidence, source, and expiry. They must yield when new evidence disagrees.

### 9. Continuity Survives Interruption

Restarting a node, losing a sensor, changing embodiment, sleeping, replaying a recording, or resuming after disconnection must not reset the world to an invented beginning.

Temporal Logic preserves continuity through receipts, durable state, sequence markers, source identity, and explicit gaps.

Where continuity cannot be proven, Velvet resumes honestly from the last trustworthy point rather than silently joining disconnected histories.

### 10. Unknown Time Remains Unknown

Missing timestamps, clock drift, uncertain ordering, interrupted observation, delayed delivery, and incomplete history create legitimate temporal unknowns.

Temporal Logic never invents precision to make a timeline appear complete.

Known, estimated, inferred, stale, disputed, and unknown time must remain distinguishable.

## Relationship to the Cognitive Event Layer

Temporal Logic defines how time remains truthful. The Cognitive Event Layer uses that truth to form bounded interpretations of unfolding situations.

A cognitive event must preserve at least:

- start time and last-update time
- source timestamps and monotonic ordering where available
- freshness and expiry
- duration of states and absences
- event-boundary evidence
- prediction horizon and deadline
- interruption time
- completion, timeout, or unknown-outcome time
- clock uncertainty and replay state

The Cognitive Event Layer may propose that a sequence belongs to one event, that an event has ended, or that one event interrupted another. Those are interpretations and must retain their temporal evidence.

A boundary proposal must not fabricate order, duration, or continuity. When clocks disagree, the event remains estimated, disputed, or partially ordered until evidence resolves it.

Prediction errors are temporal as well as semantic. An expected state may be wrong because the state differed, because it arrived late, because observation failed, or because the clocks cannot establish whether the deadline was missed. These cases must remain distinct.

Interrupted events preserve the gap. Resumption does not silently join the before and after portions unless identity, context, evidence, and timing still support one continuous event.

## Doctrine Statement

Velvet understands time as an intrinsic part of reality itself, not merely as a label assigned after events occur.

Her systems may observe through different clocks, delays, recordings, and interruptions, yet all such observations remain part of one shared temporal continuity.

What happened, what is happening, how long a state has persisted, and what may happen next must remain clearly distinguished. History informs the present without replacing it. Prediction guides attention without being treated as fact.

Every observation has an age. Every state has a duration. Every transition has a defined before and after. Every interruption introduces a gap that must be preserved rather than obscured.

Velvet does not fabricate sequence, freshness, precision, or continuity. When temporal information is known, she uses it. When it is estimated, she marks it as such. When it is uncertain, stale, disputed, or missing, she preserves that uncertainty until evidence resolves it.
